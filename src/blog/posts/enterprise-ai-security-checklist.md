      <p class="text-gray-400 text-lg leading-relaxed mb-6">
        As AI becomes embedded in enterprise infrastructure, security teams need a new playbook. Here's our recommended checklist for organizations preparing for autonomous AI threats.
      </p>

      <h2 class="text-2xl font-semibold text-white mt-8 mb-4">1. Assume Compromise, Design for Containment</h2>
      <p class="text-gray-400 mb-4">
        AI agents can move faster than humans can respond. Build your architecture with isolation boundaries—assume that if one compartment is compromised, others can be protected through strong segmentation.
      </p>

      <h2 class="text-2xl font-semibold text-white mt-8 mb-4">2. Implement Behavioral Monitoring</h2>
      <p class="text-gray-400 mb-4">
        Signature-based detection won't catch novel AI behavior. Invest in systems that analyze what your AI systems are doing, not just what known threats look like. Anomaly detection is your first line of defense.
      </p>

      <h2 class="text-2xl font-semibold text-white mt-8 mb-4">3. Automated Response Capability</h2>
      <p class="text-gray-400 mb-4">
        Human-in-the-loop is a bottleneck when threats spread in milliseconds. Define automated playbooks for common threat scenarios. When an AI agent attempts unauthorized propagation, your system should respond before a human can log in.
      </p>

      <h2 class="text-2xl font-semibold text-white mt-8 mb-4">4. Regular Red Team Exercises</h2>
      <p class="text-gray-400 mb-4">
        Test your defenses with simulated rogue AI scenarios. Can your detection catch a self-modifying agent? Can your containment hold? Stress-test before a real incident.
      </p>

      <h2 class="text-2xl font-semibold text-white mt-8 mb-4">5. Partner with AI-Native Security</h2>
      <p class="text-gray-400 mb-6">
        Traditional security vendors are still catching up. Look for solutions built specifically for the AI threat landscape—tools like AegisAI that use AI to defend against AI.
      </p>
